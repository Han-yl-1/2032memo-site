The Earth in One Second & Bookkeeping Rules (Sections 0–1)

Section 0 · How to Keep Books for More Than 8.1 Billion People: Person-Seconds, Fourteen Steps, Four Grades of Evidence

More than 8.1 billion people are alive at the same moment. Every second they are clothing, feeding, housing, and moving themselves, producing and creating, also discarding and giving up; every second new lives are born, and broken, aged lives depart. No one can see all of this at once. The woman who keeps the household accounts at the dinner table cannot see the whole family either; she keeps in mind how many days the rice jar will last, that the medicine must be renewed next month, that the child's shoes are too small again......, where next month's food money will come from. Keep the books her way, and replace the people sitting at the table with more than 8.1 billion.

This ledger does only one thing: it records everything that happens in human civilization, per unit of time, as accounts. Births, deaths, grain eaten, clothes worn out, houses built, roads traveled, oil burned, money earned, shifts worked, jobs lost, screens scrolled, words written: all go into the books. Where a cell's figure is small, first look at whose bowl that cell is; where a cell's figure is large, see who has carried an extra load.

In one second a person's body burns close to a hundred joules on average, to keep body temperature, a heartbeat, and a self that does not fall apart. A city, a server room, a rocket is the same: it trades energy for a small piece of order, and the energy used turns into heat, is dispersed, and cannot be taken back. The universe's total ledger records only toward disorder; humanity inside it is a small local patch of tidiness, bought by pushing more disorder outside itself. Every cell in the ledger is the reading of an exchange; behind the reading is who is guarding that small patch of tidiness, and how tiring the guarding is.

Units of record are unified, time scales are unified, grades of evidence are unified. With the three unified, ordinary people can do the arithmetic too.

Why count in seconds

The second can put things of different rates into the same table. 4.2 people are born per second, 1.98 die, 0.038 die on the roads, and global GDP is about $3.74 million per second: the dimensions differ, but each can be written as "how much per second," and the speed of each is visible at a glance.

Seconds can be added upward without loss. From a second to a hundred years, and from a year back down to a second, multiplication and division are all it takes. As long as the basis is right, any figure can be converted back and forth among the fourteen steps.

Three conversion rules:

  1. A year is taken as 365.2425 days (the mean Gregorian year). When a statistical agency divides a given year's annual quantity by that year's number of days (366 for 2024) to get a "daily average," it will differ from the ledger's daily average by about two parts in a thousand. This is not an error; it is a difference of basis.
  2. Month = year ÷ 12, quarter = year ÷ 4. Months and quarters are average months and average quarters and do not reflect seasonal swings. For strongly seasonal quantities (grain harvests, heating energy, tourism), the peaks and troughs are noted in each section.
  3. Every step of 5 years and above = the current annual rate × the number of years. This is a mechanical extrapolation of "keep going at today's speed," not a forecast. Population will change, technology will change, wars will stop and will also start. Population uses the UN medium variant, year by year; other flows have no scenarios and are only mechanically extrapolated.

Three kinds of quantity: stocks, flows, parameters

CategoryDefinitionExamplesCan it be spread across the fourteen steps?
StockA quantity existing at one moment8.162 billion people alive; 186 million unemployedNo. A stock has no "per second"
FlowA quantity occurring per unit of time132.4 million births a year; 62.39 million deaths a yearYes
ParameterA ratio, mean, or coefficientLife expectancy 73.3 years; unemployment rate 4.9%No; it takes part in conversions

Mixing stocks and flows is the most common error in books of this kind. "There are 186 million unemployed worldwide" is a stock; "how many people become unemployed per second" cannot be obtained by dividing a stock, and needs data on inflows into and outflows from unemployment, which no global statistic provides. When the ledger meets such a case, it writes "no statistics" and does not force a division.

There are also three further marks. The first two are the ledger's two ways of accounting for "no figure":

  • No statistics: no institution anywhere in the world has ever counted on this basis. Many are middle links in a chain: a chicken's hatching eggs, the yarn and cloth of a garment, the number of collisions before one car crash; there are also gray populations, thieves, sex workers, professional gamblers; and there are the indicators required by the system, not yet in existence, described at the end of the ledger, which humanity has not yet begun to count.
  • Unverified: some institution has counted, or some countries or some commercial bases have a figure, but the ledger has not yet pieced together a credible global value. This is a debt owed.
  • Overlapping: for example, tobacco-attributable deaths and lung-cancer deaths contain each other and cannot be added.

Appendix Table D lists the first two kinds separately: first "unverified" (those that can be filled in), then "no statistics" (those that must wait until humanity goes and counts them).

How figures are written

  • From ten thousand up, use "ten thousand," "hundred million," and "trillion," kept to three significant figures: 132 million, 171,000, $118 trillion.
  • Numeric tables keep four significant figures or the original table value, to allow recalculation.
  • Per-second quantities of less than one keep three significant figures and are also written as intervals: 0.0377 people per second = one person every 26.5 seconds.
  • Years are always stated. Where an indicator has several years, take the latest; old values appear only to show change.

The figures are taken from public institutional statistics. Every figure in a table carries its year, grade, and source, and can be traced back one by one.

Kilocalories, H₂O, and the Z-score

Energy: chemical energy

The energy of the carbon-based is the chemical energy stored in the chemical bonds of food molecules. Cells oxidize the fuel; the energy released is first stored in ATP (adenosine triphosphate), then used to contract muscle, conduct nerves, and maintain body temperature.

The whole carbon-based agent repeats a single action: it spends energy to pile things up on one side (a gradient), holds them back with a conditional door (a gate), and when the door opens they flow down the drop (a flow), doing work or passing a signal as they flow. From entry to heat, a portion of chemical energy passes three drops: electrons fall from sugar to oxygen, a potential difference of about 1.14 volts; protons fall from the mitochondrial intermembrane space back into the matrix, about 150–200 millivolts; Na⁺ falls from outside the cell into the neuron, a driving force of about 130 millivolts. ATP is the belt that carries the energy of one drop to the next.
