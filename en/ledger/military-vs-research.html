Taxes

The WDI measure of "tax revenue as a share of GDP" is 13.82% (2024). This covers central-government taxes only, and country coverage is incomplete, so it is not the government's total revenue. Adding social contributions, property income, and similar items, central-government revenue (excluding grants) comes to 23.9% of GDP (2024, WDI, A); the global weighted figure for general government revenue, which also includes local governments, remains unverified.

What This Ratio Is Worth

The sensitivity table above does only arithmetic and makes no judgment. Judgment requires knowing what one research person-year buys back, and that figure is in the section on life extension at the end of the ledger:

InputReturnBandGrade
1 research person-yearabout 1,627 person-years of life1,470–1,790C
1 student person-yearabout 2–3 person-years of life (dividing totals directly); about 16 counting only the route through researchers2–3; 14–17C

Origin: life expectancy since 1900 up by 41.17 years × 8.162 billion people = about 336 billion person-years, divided by the roughly 207 million cumulative research person-years since 1900.

This figure is unsound in four places: correlation is not causation; counting only lifespan undercounts other things; the number of researchers in 1900 is an assumption of order of magnitude; the conversion rate is on a net-increase basis. The four are set out in the paragraph "Four Loopholes" in the section on life extension, and anyone citing the multiple must cite them with it.

Connect it back to this section's ratio: today research takes 3.6 in 10,000 of all human time. The scorecard at the end of the ledger singles out this ratio as the one master-gate indicator, because it is at once the multiplier at the efficiency end and one of the terms at the intergenerational-fairness end.
